# Mission France Miniature — teacher guide

Students download **index.html** and open it in a regular browser. It works offline, with **no ChatGPT account, student login, installation or external service**. The seven supplied meme reactions and the PDF generator are inside the HTML file.

The complete original reading passage is preserved word for word. Its 24 present/passé composé groups are interactive: 12 present and 12 passé composé, including repeated occurrences. *Regarde* (twice), *Imagine* and the infinitives remain in the passage as plain text. The recognition task includes the original irregular participles and final passive construction; these are identified, not taught as production models.

The construction tasks and the **expanded exit ticket use avoir and selected regular participle models only**. No exit item requires *être*, an irregular participle, negation or agreement. With the new side quest, allow around **70–80 minutes**, or split the activity into two lessons. Keep the matching and the individual ticket even if the quest is assigned for a second session.

## Lesson flow

| Time | Activity | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 min | Open the file; enter first name/initials, class and optional partner. | One saved attempt. |
| 11 min | Read the full text; classify all 24 underlined verb groups. | First answers, corrections and hint use. |
| 6 min | Identify subject, auxiliary and participle; change the subject. | Four construction questions. |
| 9 min | Match six infinitives to their participles, then match three infinitive-family models to their participle endings. | Six word associations followed by three ending associations, in that order. |
| 18–22 min | Operation Néon: forge 20 typed participles, then send 20 messages using those same verbs with avoir. | 40 typed responses, four relay stations, hints and every retry recorded. |
| 10 min | Complete six monument challenges in a chosen order. | Three repair tasks and three typed completions. |
| 10–12 min | Complete the individual exit ticket, then correct selected mistakes. | Twelve questions across five exercise types, with every submitted response recorded. |
| 5 min | Debrief, download the PDF and submit it to the teacher. | A full report with expected answers and attempt history. |

In pairs, use a **reader/explainer** and a **pilot**. Swap at each stage and after three monument challenges. Each student should use their own file for an individual record. On a shared device, training is a shared record: put both names on it and collect separate paper exit tickets using the questions below.

## Discovery stays before production

The **Associer** stage has two distinct matching boards. Students first select an infinitive on the left and its past participle on the right. The six pairs are *visiter/visité, regarder/regardé, choisir/choisi, finir/fini, attendre/attendu,* and *vendre/vendu*. It uses clickable, keyboard-accessible buttons, so no dragging is required. Each correct match moves out of the shuffled board into an aligned infinitive → participle row. After completion, only the correct paired rows remain; the ending matches behave the same way.

Only after those six pairs are correct does the second board open. Students match the three observed models to **-é, -i and -u**. The complete pattern summary appears after all three ending associations are validated. The wording makes clear that the selected *-ir* and *-re* models do not apply to every verb with those infinitive endings.

## The cyberpunk side quest: Operation Néon

Four relays connect the miniature monuments. Students can choose their order; each relay holds five verbs. A restrained dark terminal, circuit diagram, mint/violet accents and restored-relay counters provide the cyberpunk style. There is no timer, sound, roleplay or extra setup.

1. **Forge the chips:** students type the past participles of 20 different verbs. There are no multiple-choice answers. All 20 must be validated before phase 2 opens.
2. **Transmit the messages:** students reuse those same 20 verbs in contextual sentences, typing **avoir + past participle**. Subjects rotate through *je, tu, il/elle/on, nous, vous,* and *ils/elles*.

The set contains eight *-er*, six regular *-ir* and six model *-re* verbs:

| -er → -é | -ir → -i | -re → -u |
| --- | --- | --- |
| visiter → visité | finir → fini | attendre → attendu |
| regarder → regardé | choisir → choisi | vendre → vendu |
| photographier → photographié | réussir → réussi | répondre → répondu |
| admirer → admiré | remplir → rempli | perdre → perdu |
| écouter → écouté | réfléchir → réfléchi | entendre → entendu |
| explorer → exploré | agir → agi | rendre → rendu |
| préparer → préparé | | |
| chercher → cherché | | |

Every one of these verbs uses **avoir** in its supplied sentence. No irregular participle or *être* construction is introduced in the side quest. Accents count; reference characters are available to copy. Feedback and the supplied memes accompany responses, and hints are recorded. A correct answer stays visible until the student loads the next code. Completed questions cannot add extra credit when revisited.

The PDF and CSV include all 40 quest tasks, their expected answers and every submitted answer. The PDF summary also separates **participles written /20** and **avoir transmissions /20**. The full practice bank now totals **83 items**: 24 reading groups, 4 assembly questions, 6 word matches, 3 ending matches, 40 quest tasks and 6 monument challenges. The 12-item individual exit ticket remains separate.

For two sessions: finish recognition, assembly and discovery in the first; use the side quest, monument challenges and individual ticket in the second. On a shorter day, the navigation permits moving to the ticket; unattempted quest items remain visible as unattempted in the report.

## The longer exit ticket

The structure follows the Verb Heist approach: students review all their draft answers and submit them together **before any feedback appears**. It adds varied tasks and a separate correction pass.

| Section | Items | Task |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 2 | Choose the correct present-tense form of avoir. |
| B | 3 | Form a regular past participle. |
| C | 3 | Type auxiliary + past participle into a sentence. |
| D | 2 | Rewrite a sentence to repair an auxiliary or participle error. |
| E | 2 | Change a full sentence from present to passé composé. |

**First pass:** work alone without notes, the other stages or a partner. This is the classroom instruction; the file is not an examination lockdown tool. Draft changes are saved but do not count as submitted attempts. All 12 answers must be filled before the first submission.

**Correction pass:** the first score stays fixed. Correct items are locked. Students may retry incorrect items individually. Feedback appears after the first submission; after two incorrect submissions to an item, the expected model is displayed. Later responses are marked “after model.” Students can finish with unresolved errors: the report records them accurately and includes the expected response.

**Finish:** click **Terminer et télécharger mon rapport PDF**. The ticket becomes final and the browser starts a real `.pdf` download. Students check their downloads and submit that file through the school's usual channel. The report can be downloaded again from **Mon bilan**. Printing/saving as PDF remains available as a fallback.

Meme reactions appear with learning feedback, not in the teacher report. French accents count; casing, straight/curly apostrophes, extra spaces and final punctuation are tolerated.

## What the PDF contains

The PDF is generated on the student's device and has an A4 layout with repeated table headers and page numbers. It includes:

- Student/class/partner, attempt date and completion status.
- The initial individual score and the score after corrections, kept separate.
- Totals for submitted responses, incorrect responses and retries.
- Results by each of the five exit exercises, plus a separate training summary.
- Every exit question and all 83 training questions, including unattempted items.
- The expected answer and **every submitted student answer in order**, with its outcome and conditions.
- For each question: **tries / incorrect answers / retries**.
- Learner notes and a suggested next step.

One **try** is one submitted answer to one question. A **retry** is an additional try after its first. A draft edit is neither. There is no extra credit for repeating a completed item. Training hints, correction feedback and displayed models are distinguished. The PDF grows when students make more attempts; it preserves the evidence instead of silently dropping earlier mistakes.

The CSV contains **one row per submitted answer**, with question ID, expected answer, response number, actual response, outcome, feedback/model condition, timestamp and per-question totals. Unattempted questions get a clearly marked row. It uses UTF-8 and semicolons.

**Example_Student_Report.pdf** uses fictitious answers to demonstrate an 8/12 first pass becoming 11/12 after corrections. It shows the incorrect forms, the corrections and their attempt counts. It is a preview, not a student result.

There is **no central dashboard or automatic submission**. Downloading the PDF does not send it to the teacher.

### B. Write the past participle only

3. visiter → ____________________
4. finir → ____________________

6. Tu ____________________ un château. *(visiter)*
7. Nous ____________________ nos billets. *(choisir)*

### D. Correct and rewrite the complete sentence

9. Vous a regardé le spectacle.

________________________________________________________________

________________________________________________________________

### E. Change the whole sentence to passé composé

11. Je photographie la tour Eiffel.

________________________________________________________________

________________________________________________________________

## Reuse and later editing

Download and open the HTML in a browser: school-platform previews sometimes block scripts. Test once on a school device, especially local-file access and downloads. If direct downloading is blocked by school settings, use **Imprimer / enregistrer en PDF**.

Saved attempts live in that browser when local storage is available. Export before changing devices or moving the file. For a new student or replay, export first and then use **Pour l’enseignant → Nouvelle tentative**. This clears the current local attempt. Existing training progress is preserved when upgrading from the old three-question ticket; the previous short-ticket result is retained in local storage as an archive, while the new final ticket starts fresh.

**Neon_Side_Quest_Bank.json** mirrors the side-quest verbs, contexts, auxiliary forms and ending matches. Its source in the HTML is **NEON_BANK**, with the nearby **endingPatterns** data. As with the exit-ticket JSON, editing the mirror alone does not update the game.

The editable **EXIT_BANK** block in the HTML contains the sections, verb metadata, prompts, choices, expected answers and feedback. **Exit_Ticket_Question_Bank.json** is a readable mirror for planning later changes; editing that JSON alone does not change the standalone game. Update the HTML block as well, or provide the edited JSON for a later revision. Use new item IDs when changing question meanings so old attempts are not attributed to different questions.

The current bank contains *photographier, admirer, visiter, finir, attendre, choisir, vendre,* and *regarder*. When adding irregulars or *être* later, update the question bank, scope label, examples and feedback together. The history/report engine already uses the same data for expected answers and can accept explicit alternative answers via an `accepted` list.
